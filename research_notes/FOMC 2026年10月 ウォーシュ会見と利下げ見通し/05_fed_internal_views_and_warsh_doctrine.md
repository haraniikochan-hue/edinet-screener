# Fed Internal Views and the Warsh Doctrine (as of October 28, 2026)

**Critical framing note for the report writer.** The assignment was written on the premise that the Fed is debating "further cuts." The sourced record for 2026 shows the opposite: an energy shock from the U.S.–Iran war (which began in late February 2026) pushed headline inflation back up, the FOMC *raised* rates by 25 bp on September 16, 2026 (unanimous, to 3.75–4.00%, the first hike since July 2023), and at today's October 28 meeting it *held* at 3.75–4.00% in a 10–2 vote with both dissents favoring a *hike*. The September SEP median implies one more hike in 2026, and markets price a December hike. The scenario analysis for the next 6–12 months should therefore be framed as "how many more hikes, when, and what would stop them," not "when do cuts resume." Every claim below is dated and sourced; items I could not verify are listed under Gaps.

**Method caveats.** (1) Direct page fetches (federalreserve.gov, cnbc.com, etc.) were blocked by this session's network policy, so quotations come from search-engine summaries of those pages, not from the full documents; the minutes quotations below match across multiple independent outlets but should be re-checked against the official minutes before publication. **Pre- vs post-meeting.** Everything in the "October 28 decision" section is *post*-meeting (today). All speeches/interviews cited are *pre*-meeting (blackout ran from roughly Oct 17); no official other than Warsh has spoken since the decision.

### Inferences
- With two hawkish dissents, a statement that explicitly ties the next step to "accumulation of more information," and a Chair who says conditions are "not yet tight," the center of gravity points to a December hike unless the October data (PCE Oct 29, October CPI/jobs) break lower. The hold looks like a timing choice (midterms six days away, Warsh's stated dislike of "pre-judging") rather than a change in direction.
- Cook's dissent matters beyond this meeting: it shows the hawkish camp now includes a Biden-appointed governor who is simultaneously the target of a White House removal process, which makes it very hard for the administration to frame the Board's hawkishness as "political."

## Key Question 1: September 15–16, 2026 FOMC minutes (released October 7, 2026)

### Takeaway
The minutes show a unanimous hike (12–0) but a divided rationale: "many" saw a higher path as risk-management insurance, "some" saw the hike as containing energy-shock pass-through, "a couple" tied it to a higher neutral rate, and "most" expected another hike by year-end, while "several" said policy was still "not restrictive or only mildly restrictive." Labor was characterized as near maximum employment with balanced risks; financial conditions as supportive; balance-sheet discussion was minimal.

### Inferences
- The "precautionary vs. regime-tightening" split is the key fault line for 2027: the "precautionary" camp (plausibly Williams, Jefferson, Bowman, Paulson) can stop after one or two more hikes if energy pass-through fades; the "not restrictive" camp (Hammack, Logan, Musalem, Kashkari, Waller, Barr, now Cook) implies a terminal rate meaningfully above 4%.
- Nothing in the reported minutes suggests a framework or inflation-target change was discussed in September; those questions have been routed to Warsh's task forces (see Q6).

- Whether any participant argued for no hike in September (the "a few" language) could not be verified.

## Key Question 4: Dissent history in 2026 and the committee's center of gravity

### Takeaway
2026 has produced the most dissents in a year since at least 2008, and since April every dissent has been *hawkish*: four in April (three against the statement's easing bias, one by Miran for a cut), three in July (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan for a hike), none at the unanimous September hike, and two today (Cook, Hammack for a hike). The center of gravity has moved decisively toward tightening and the hawkish wing keeps pulling.

## Key Question 8: Balance sheet status and Warsh's plans

### Takeaway
QT ended December 1, 2025 and the Fed began "reserve management purchases" of T-bills in December 2025 (roughly $120 bn through mid-April per a Fed note), lifting total assets to about $6.75 trillion by August 2026. Year-end 2025 saw record SRF use ($74.6 bn) and SOFR above the target range on some days. Warsh wants a smaller, shorter portfolio and a new Treasury accord, but the committee has deferred to the Dynan/Rajan/Stein task force (report due year-end), and the 10-year above 5% has made near-term shrinkage inopportune.

### Cited Findings
- Balance-sheet runoff ended effective December 1, 2025, and RMPs began in December 2025 to keep reserves ample — [NY Fed Perli speech Mar 26](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2026/per260326); [NY Fed Perli July 9](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2026/per260709)
- RMPs totaled about $120 bn through mid-April 2026; repo rates eased several bp as MMFs shifted from bills to repo — [Fed FEDS Note Aug 26](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/repo-markets-and-the-feds-balance-sheet-implications-for-monetary-policy-implementation-20260826.html); an alternative estimate is ~$40 bn Dec–Apr — [EconReporter](https://en.econreporter.com/57516/fed-standing-repo-facility-watch/); Wolf Street tracker — [Wolf Street Mar 5](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/03/05/update-on-the-feds-balance-sheet-and-its-reserve-management-purchases/)
- QT cut assets by ~$2.4 tn to ~$6.5 tn by end-2025; RMPs (~$40 bn/month of bills) pushed total assets back to $6.75 tn by August 2026 — [First Trust Aug 13](https://www.ftportfolios.com/Commentary/EconomicResearch/2026/8/13/can-warsh-shrink-the-feds-balance-sheet); [Equiti](https://www.equiti.com/sc-en/news/global-macro-analysis/kevin-warsh-fed-balance-sheet-reserves-qt/)
- Repo stress: SRF usage hit a record $74.6 bn on Dec 31, 2025 and $30 bn on one day in early 2026 (third-largest ever); the Fed removed the SRF aggregate daily limit after the December meeting; SOFR/TGCR printed 4.12%/4.08% on one Friday, above the range; year-end pressures were "substantial but short-lived" — [YX Insights](https://www.yxinsights.com/p/standing-repo-facility); [Fed March minutes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20260318.htm); [EconReporter](https://en.econreporter.com/57516/fed-standing-repo-facility-watch/); [NY Fed Teller Window](https://tellerwindow.newyorkfed.org/monetary-policy-implementation)
- Administered rates after the September hike (lower-quality source): IORB 3.90%, ON RRP 3.75%, SRF 4.00%, EFFR 3.88% — [MacroOdds](https://macroodds.com/blog/federal-reserve-interest-rates-2026)
- Fed research: repo-market capacity to absorb Treasuries rolling off is the key limit on how far the balance sheet can shrink — [FEDS working paper 2026-019](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026019pap.pdf)
- July minutes: a balance-sheet discussion "would be a useful input into Committee deliberations" and future meetings "would provide an opportunity for a comprehensive discussion"; voters reluctant to move before the task force reports — [Fed July minutes PDF](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/fomcminutes20260729.pdf); [CNBC Sept 25](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/kevin-warsh-fed-interest-rates-balance-sheet.html)
- Analyst expectations: a modestly smaller balance sheet from Q4 2026 at the earliest, holding Treasuries steady while continuing the run-down of ~$2 tn MBS; hybrid outcome of a leaner balance sheet plus standing facilities as permanent backstops — [ABN AMRO](https://www.abnamro.com/research/en/our-research/kevin-warshs-reverse-operation-twist); [Equiti](https://www.equiti.com/sc-en/news/global-macro-analysis/kevin-warsh-fed-balance-sheet-reserves-qt/); [American Banker opinion](https://www.americanbanker.com/opinion/before-shrinking-the-feds-balance-sheet-warsh-has-other-work-to-do); [Honest Economist](https://www.honesteconomist.com/column/warsh-fed-balance-sheet-review)
- TradingKey: Warsh era "signals a death knell for QE" — [TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/economic/central-banks/261567407-kevin-warsh-fed-bessent-qt-rate-cut-treasury-tradingkey)

### Inferences
- The balance sheet is currently *growing* (RMPs), not shrinking, which is in tension with Warsh's stated aims; the practical constraint is reserve scarcity (repo stress at year-end 2025 and early 2026) and the 5% 10-year. A plausible 2027 sequence is: task-force report (Dec 2026) → announced framework (early 2027, possibly at the new strategy-only meeting) → resumed runoff or portfolio shortening later in 2027. Any such move would tighten financial conditions at the long end and could reduce the number of policy-rate hikes needed — a substitution the writer should model explicitly.
- A new Treasury–Fed accord that gives Treasury a say over the balance sheet would be read by markets as reduced independence even if the near-term policy effect is hawkish.

## Key Question 9: What the Fed is most worried about, and how it maps to the rate path

### Takeaway
The dominant worry is inflation re-accelerating via an energy shock that is spreading into broader prices and expectations, compounded by AI-investment demand and tariffs, with policy only "mildly restrictive" at 4%. Labor-market cracks (payrolls +29k, unemployment 4.2%) are acknowledged but not yet policy-relevant; financial-stability concerns (AI valuations, 5% long yields) and independence/fiscal-dominance risks are secondary but shape *how* (spaced, un-guided) rather than *whether* the Fed tightens.

### Cited Findings
- Macro backdrop: the U.S.–Iran conflict began in late February 2026 "and shows no signs of ending anytime soon"; September CPI 3.7% y/y (Aug 3.4%), gasoline +9.1% accounting for half the increase; tariffs, AI data-center construction and travel demand also cited — [CNBC Oct 14](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/cpi-inflation-report-september-2026.html); [CNBC breakdown](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/cpi-inflation-breakdown-september-2026.html); [Wikipedia: Economic impact of the 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_2026_Iran_war)

### Inferences (scenario inputs for the next 6–12 months)
- **Base case (consistent with SEP median, minutes "most participants," and today's statement): one more 25 bp hike at the December 8–9 meeting to 4.00–4.25%, then an extended hold through mid-2027** with Warsh resisting guidance on the terminal rate. Triggers: September PCE (Oct 29) at or above nowcast (~3.6%/3.05%), October CPI, and no sharp labor deterioration.
- **Hawkish scenario ("not restrictive" camp prevails): two more hikes (December and Q1 2027) to 4.25–4.50%,** driven by continued energy pass-through, expectations drift, or AI-demand inflation; supported by Hammack/Logan/Musalem/Waller/Barr/Cook and by the four participants with two-hike dots. The loss of Hammack/Logan/Kashkari votes in January makes a Q1 hike somewhat less likely than a December one.
- **Dovish-tail scenario: December skip and no further hikes** if the labor market cracks widen (payroll trend near zero, unemployment >4.4%) or if an Iran ceasefire collapses oil prices; even then, no speaker is on record favoring cuts, so cuts before late 2027 would require a visible disinflation plus a growth scare.
- **Structural wild cards:** (1) task-force reports in December could change the SEP/dot plot and announce a balance-sheet framework; a 2027 shift to portfolio shortening would tighten long rates and could reduce the needed policy-rate increases; (2) the Cook inquiry (Nov 5 hearing) could produce a removal attempt and new litigation, reviving independence concerns; (3) Treasury buybacks vs. Fed balance-sheet shrinkage is the fiscal-dominance flashpoint to watch.
