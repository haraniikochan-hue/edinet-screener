## CRITICAL FRAMING NOTE FOR THE REPORT WRITER (read first)

The assignment framed the scenarios as "continued cuts each meeting / pause in December then resume cuts / extended pause / re-hike." **The actual policy regime is the opposite: the Warsh Fed is in a *hiking* cycle.** The Fed raised rates 25bp on September 16, 2026 (first hike since July 2023), held at 3.75–4.00% today with two dissents *in favor of a hike*, and markets price a ~85–90% chance of a **hike** on December 9. No major house forecasts a cut before mid-2027; the dovish tail is "no more hikes," not "cuts." The scenario set should therefore be re-cast as:

- (A) December hike, then extended hold at 4.00–4.25% (sell-side modal view)
- (B) December + March 2027 hikes to 4.25–4.50%, then hold
- (C) Three or more further hikes to ~4.6–4.7% by H2 2027 (what futures/OIS imply)
- (D) December skip and extended hold at 3.75–4.00% (market-implied tail ~10–15%)
- (E) Cuts resume in 2027 (several houses: Citi from June 2027, Bloomberg Economics, Morningstar H2 2027, Goldman/UBS late 2027)

Details and sourcing for each are below; a suggested probability mapping is in the final section.

## Key Question 1: What happened today, what was priced going in, and was it a surprise?

### Takeaway
A hold at 3.75–4.00% was ~90%+ priced and delivered; the hawkish elements (10–2 vote with Cook and Hammack dissenting for +25bp, statement still calling inflation "elevated") pushed December-hike odds *up* to ~85%, but Warsh's refusal to pre-commit and his meeting-schedule announcement were read as mildly dovish, so 2-year yields fell ~4bp during the press conference. Net: a "hawkish hold" roughly as previewed, with a modest dovish tilt in the presser relative to the statement.

### Inferences
- The outcome was essentially as previewed ("hawkish hold"). The hawkish surprise was the two dissents (statement/vote), which pushed December odds up ~7pp; the dovish surprise was the presser (no commitment, "conditions have tightened," meeting-schedule change), which pulled the 2-year down ~4bp. Net surprise: small, slightly hawkish on December, slightly dovish on the 2027 path.
- The July precedent (dovish-sounding presser followed by a September hike) suggests the writer should not over-weight the dovish tone of today's presser relative to the dissents and the data.
- A "Warsh put" (in the sense of a bias to ease when markets wobble) is not the market's read: his comment that tighter financial conditions are "helping create a path toward lower inflation" implies he welcomes tighter conditions, i.e., the reaction function treats market tightening as a substitute for hikes rather than a trigger for easing.

### Inferences
- Nominal neutral ≈ 3.0% (HLW) to 3.2–3.25% (FOMC). A December hike takes the midpoint to 4.125%, ~90–110bp above neutral; the market's ~4.6–4.7% would be ~140–170bp above. This is why nearly every house sees the cycle ending at 4.00–4.50% and why the Fed's own dots have cuts starting in 2028 (3.9% end-2028).
- Plausible cut depth once easing begins (2028 in the dots; late 2027 in GS/UBS/Citi): back toward ~3.25–3.5%, i.e., 100–125bp from a 4.25–4.50% peak.
