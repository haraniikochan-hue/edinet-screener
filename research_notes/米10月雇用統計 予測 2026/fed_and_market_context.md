## Q1. What did the FOMC decide on Oct 28, 2026, what did the statement/press conference say about labor, what is the target range, and what did the September SEP show?

### Takeaway
The FOMC held at 3.75-4.00% on Oct 28 by 10-2 (Cook and Hammack dissented FOR a 25bp hike), said "job gains have kept pace with the growth of the workforce, and the unemployment rate has changed little," and declared "inflation remains elevated... The Committee will deliver price stability." Warsh called the employment side "quite positive" and said the meeting "focused mainly on the price stability side"; the September SEP median had 2026 fed funds at 4.1% (one more hike) and unemployment at 4.1% with 17 of 18 seeing balanced unemployment risks.

### Cited Findings

The Committee will deliver price stability." Vote 10-2; Governors Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. ### Inferences
- The Fed's reaction function is inflation-first. The labor market is treated as a permissive condition for hiking, not a reason to pause; only a clear deterioration (unemployment breaking out of the 4.1-4.2% range, negative payrolls with downward revisions) would plausibly block December. A modest print leaves the inflation data (Oct CPI Nov 10, Oct PCE Nov 25) as the real swing factor.
- Warsh's refusal to pre-commit plus the two hawkish dissents means the committee's center of gravity is already at "hike in December"; Friday's data is more likely to shift the 2027 path (second hike timing) than the December decision itself.

### Gaps
- The transcript is marked PRELIMINARY; the final text could differ slightly. The press conference did not discuss "low-hire, low-fire" or quantify payroll breakeven.
- Kiplinger's live blog snippet claiming a "unanimous" vote to "raise" is wrong versus the Fed's own release; disregarded.

## Q2. Who is Fed Chair, and what have Fed officials said in late Oct / early Nov 2026 about the labor market and what would change their vote?

### Takeaway
