## Key Question 1: Is the October 2026 jobs report confirmed for Nov 6, 2026 08:30 ET? Any BLS notices (shutdown, delays, methodology, data collection)?

### Takeaway
Yes. The BLS schedule lists the October 2026 Employment Situation for **Friday, November 6, 2026, 8:30 AM ET (22:30 JST)** with no delay/postponement notice; the September report itself (released on time on Oct 2, 2026) also pointed to Nov 6. The federal government is funded under a continuing resolution through **December 11, 2026**, so no shutdown affects this release.

### Cited Findings
- BLS Employment Situation schedule: October 2026 data → Nov 06, 2026, 08:30 AM; November 2026 → Dec 04, 2026; December 2026 → Jan 08, 2027; January 2027 → Feb 05, 2027. - The FY2027 continuing resolution (Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, H.R. 6500) was signed in early September 2026 and funds the government at FY2026 levels **through December 11, 2026**; the House vote was 370–48 — [AACOM, 2026-09-08](https://www.aacom.org/news-reports/news/2026/09/08/president-trump-signs-continuing-resolution-to-fund-government-through-december-11); [Bloomberg Government bill analysis](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/bgov-bill-analysis-fiscal-2027-stopgap-funding-to-dec-11)

### Inferences
- With the CR running to Dec 11, both the Nov 6 (October data) and Dec 4 (November data) releases are outside any funding-lapse window; the next shutdown risk point would affect the Jan 8, 2027 release (December data) only if a lapse begins Dec 12 and persists.

### Gaps

## Key Question 7: Preliminary benchmark revision (March 2026) and birth-death model implications

### Takeaway
BLS's preliminary benchmark (released 2026-08-28) shows March 2026 total nonfarm employment overstated by only **−79K (−0.1%)** — private **−178K (−0.1%)**, government **+99K (+0.4%)** — far smaller than the prior year's final −862K NSA / −898K SA (March 2025). The final revision will be published with the January 2027 report in February 2027; it will not affect the Nov 6 release.

### Cited Findings
- Preliminary benchmark revision for March 2026 (USDL-26-1425, Friday 2026-08-28 10:00 ET): total nonfarm **−79,000 (−0.1%)**; total private **−178,000 (−0.1%)**; the 10-year average absolute annual revision is 0.2% of total nonfarm; the final benchmark will be published in February 2027 with the January 2027 Employment Situation; official estimates are not updated based on the preliminary figure — [BLS preliminary benchmark release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/prebmk.nr0.htm)
- Industry detail (K / %): mining & logging −6 (−1.0); construction **+62 (+0.8)**; manufacturing **−67 (−0.5)**; trade/transport/utilities −98 (−0.3) [wholesale −86.2 (−1.4); retail **−154.6 (−1.0)**; transportation & warehousing **+135.1 (+2.0)**; utilities +8.1 (+1.3)]; information +87 (+3.0); financial activities +85 (+0.9); professional & business services −76 (−0.3); private education & health −96 (−0.3); leisure & hospitality −33 (−0.2); other services −36 (−0.6); **government +99 (+0.4)** — [BLS prebmk Table 1](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/prebmk.t01.htm)
- Prior year (March 2025) final benchmark, published 2026-02-11 with the January 2026 report: NSA **−862K (−0.5%)** (157,540K vs 158,402K); SA **−898K (−0.6%)**; history of final revisions: 2019 −489K (−0.3%), 2022 +506K (+0.3%), 2023 −187K (−0.1%), 2024 **−598K (−0.4%)**, 2025 **−861K (−0.5%)** — [BLS CES benchmark article](https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cesbmart.htm)
- Birth-death model: two parts (indirect imputation for business deaths + ARIMA model for residual net birth-death). From April 2024 to March 2025 actual net birth-death was **117K below** the forecast used in monthly estimates. In the post-benchmark period Apr–Dec 2025 the model cumulatively added **917K** jobs vs 1,160K in previously published estimates. **Starting with the preliminary January 2026 estimates, BLS modified the ARIMA component to incorporate current sample information**; this cut forecasts for Apr–Oct 2025 by 185K vs the preliminary forecasts (124K lower than the usual post-benchmark forecasts); a similar adjustment in the 2024 post-benchmark period reduced the March 2025 private benchmark adjustment by 229K. BLS "will use the same modified net birth-death forecasts in the two subsequent monthly revisions without further updates" — [BLS CES benchmark article](https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cesbmart.htm)
- The preliminary benchmark page does not discuss the birth-death model explicitly; it describes the revision as the difference between CES and QCEW (UI tax record) counts from March 2025 to March 2026 — [BLS prebmk release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/prebmk.nr0.htm)

### Inferences
- The −79K preliminary revision implies CES overstated the Mar 2025–Mar 2026 payroll gain by roughly 6–7K/month — negligible versus the −72K/month implied by the 2025 benchmark (−862K/12). The much smaller miss is consistent with the birth-death model modification introduced in January 2026 having curbed the overstatement, though BLS has not (in the pages fetched) attributed it.
- The private/government split (−178K / +99K) suggests private hiring has been slightly weaker than the monthly data show and government (likely state/local) slightly stronger; retail (−154.6K) and manufacturing (−67K) are the most overstated sectors, transportation/warehousing (+135K) and information (+87K) the most understated (possibly reclassification between retail/wholesale and T&W).
- From prior knowledge (NOT re-verified in this session): the March 2025 *preliminary* benchmark announced 2025-09-09 was −911K (−0.6%); the final came in at −862K NSA. If used in the report, verify at bls.gov/news.release/archives/prebmk_09092025.htm.

### Gaps
- BLS has not published any statement on whether the Jan 2026 birth-death modification is the cause of the smaller 2026 preliminary revision; no sell-side commentary on the −79K figure was retrieved.
- The March 2026 CES level used in the preliminary benchmark table was not shown on the fetched page.
