# FOMC October 27–28, 2026: Decision, Written Materials, Vote, Balance Sheet, Market Reaction, and September-SEP Baseline

**Important framing note for the report writer.** The assignment brief assumed the October 2026 meeting was about *cuts* ("larger cut, smaller cut, hold"). The sourced record is the opposite: the FOMC **raised** the target range by 25 bp in **September 2026** (first hike since July 2023) and **held** it at **3.75–4.00%** on **October 28, 2026**, with two dissents **in favor of a further hike**. Markets and the Reuters economist poll now expect a **December 2026 hike**, not a cut. Any "利下げ見通し" (rate-cut outlook) framing must be reconciled with this.

All findings below therefore come from extended web-search result summaries and quoted snippets, not from reading full pages. ## Key Question 1: What was the decision on the federal funds target range, and what are IORB, discount rate, ON RRP (and SRF) after the meeting?

### Takeaway
The FOMC held the federal funds target range at **3-3/4 to 4 percent** (3.75–4.00%) on October 28, 2026, its first pause after a 25 bp hike in September 2026. Administered rates were left unchanged: IORB 3.90%, standing repo facility 4.00%, ON RRP 3.75% ($160 bn per-counterparty limit), primary credit (discount) rate 4.00%.

### Inferences
- With IORB at 3.90% inside a 3.75–4.00% range and ON RRP at the bottom (3.75%), the corridor configuration is unchanged from the post-September setup; the Board's IORB vote is described as unanimous even though two FOMC members dissented on the target range (the IORB vote is a Board vote, and Hammack is not a Board member; Cook apparently did not dissent on IORB — unverified beyond "voted unanimously").
- The combination "hold now, two hawkish dissents, December hike priced ~85%" means the October decision is best characterized as a pause in a tightening cycle, not a pivot.

## Key Question 3: What was the vote? Who dissented, in which direction, and why?

### Takeaway

## Key Question 5: Balance-sheet decisions — status of QT, reserve management purchases (RMPs), reinvestment policy, any new announcements

### Takeaway
No new balance-sheet policy was announced on October 28, 2026. QT (runoff) ended December 1, 2025. Reserve management purchases of T-bills began December 12, 2025 at $40 bn/month, were tapered to $25 bn then $10 bn, and have been at **zero since August 14, 2026**, with the pause extended on September 14 (to mid-October) and again on October 14 (to mid-November). Reinvestment policy continues: Treasury principal rolled over at auction; agency MBS/debt principal reinvested in T-bills. The October implementation note keeps the standing RMP authority in place.

### Cited Findings
- QT end: on October 29, 2025 the FOMC announced it would cease runoff "starting on December 1, 2025," directing the Desk to roll over all Treasury principal at auction and reinvest agency principal into Treasury bills — [Fed Oct 29, 2025 statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20251029a.htm); [First Citizens, "The Federal Reserve ends QT"](https://innovation.firstcitizens.com/market-insights/us-treasuries/the-federal-reserve-ends-qt-key-market-liquidity-insights)
- RMP start: on December 10, 2025 the Fed said reserves had "declined to ample levels" and directed bill purchases; the Desk started at $40 bn/month from December 12, then cut to $25 bn and $10 bn — [Wolf Street, Aug 13, 2026](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/08/13/fed-cuts-reserve-management-purchases-rmps-to-zero-starting-august-14/)
